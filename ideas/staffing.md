# Candidate: checkpoint staffing scenario planner

**Idea, not an operational scheduling tool.** An internal interface could help a supervisor explore how a simulated demand surge might affect checkpoint waits and compare hypothetical staffing adjustments. An initial demo would be a scenario planner with synthetic rosters, qualifications, breaks, lane capacity, and other explicit constraints; a human supervisor approves or rejects hypothetical changes. It does not automatically schedule staff or change a real roster.

The interface would explain which constraints make a proposal feasible or infeasible, show the assumed demand and capacity, and present estimated outcomes with uncertainty and clear synthetic labels. A paired demo can show a simulated wait changing after an adjustment, but the difference is a model output, not proof of a causal staffing benefit, a safe operational recommendation, or real-world performance.

Before any operational roadmap, the team would need verified source definitions, data access and approvals, applicable labor and scheduling rules, validation against observed outcomes, and responsible human review. Throughput alone does not establish staffing needs, capacity, utilization or passenger wait time.
